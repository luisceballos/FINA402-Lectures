FINA 402 · LECTURE 14 · 02 / 12

Recap: buying a call or a put

Call

The buyer pays a premium for the right to buy the underlying asset at strike K at expiration.

Put

The buyer pays a premium for the right to sell the underlying asset at strike K at expiration.

Buyer positions only · K = strike · S_T = stock price at expiration · c_t / p_t = call / put premium.

FINA 402 · LECTURE 14 · 03 / 12

Four positions: payoff and P&L

━━ Payoff   ┄┄ P&L (including premium) · Horizontal axis: stock price at expiration.

Selling reverses the buyer’s payoff and P&L. Premium shifts the P&L curve; the payoff kink remains at K.

TJUL: protection over a two-year investment

Investment strategy
Manager’s objective: keep some equity upside while seeking to protect the initial investment over two years.
Why this combination? Bought calls provide equity exposure; bought puts protect the downside; sold calls help finance protection in exchange for an upside cap. The 100% buffer target is before fees, for the full period, and is not guaranteed.

Strategy 2: Covered Call

Objective: earn premium from a stock you own when your outlook is neutral or moderately bullish. Accept an upside ceiling; this is an income strategy with equity risk, not downside insurance.

Maximum gain: $14. If the stock goes to zero, the loss is $96.

Portfolio P&L = Stock P&L + Short-call P&L.

  • Flat market: premium can help.
  • Large rally: the short call gives away upside.
  • Large decline: you still own the falling stock.

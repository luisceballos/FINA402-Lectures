Lecture 10 recap: bond price, YTM and coupon

C = semiannual coupon payment · t = time in years · T = maturity in years · FV = face value

Fixed cash flows: higher YTM → lower price.

YTM compared with the coupon rate

Bond priceYield comparison
Premium: P > FVYTM < coupon rate
Par: P = FVYTM = coupon rate
Discount: P < FVYTM > coupon rate

The coupon rate fixes the payments. YTM is the single discount rate that matches their present value to the observed price.

FINA 402 · LECTURE 11 · 06 / 16

How to read Moody’s, S&P and Fitch ratings

One notch = one step.
BBB → BBB− is one-notch downgrade.
BBB− → BB+ crosses into high yield.
Moody’s equivalent boundary: Baa3 → Ba1.

D: Default.
Default labels: S&P uses SD / D; Fitch uses RD / D. Moody’s uses its own Ca / C definitions rather than a D category.

Ratings are credit opinions, not guarantees. Outlook is a separate signal; it is not a notch.

Sources: Moody’s definitions · S&P guide · Fitch definitions

FINA 402 · LECTURE 11 · 12 / 16

What is inside the spread?

01

Default likelihood

Can operating cash flow cover debt service? Look at leverage, interest coverage and upcoming maturities.

02

Loss if default occurs

Recovery depends on collateral, seniority and the restructuring outcome.

03

Risk & liquidity

Investors also require compensation for uncertainty and difficulty selling the bond.

Weaker cash flow or refinancing pressure → investors may demand a wider spread → today’s bond value falls.

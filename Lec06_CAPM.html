01

Efficient frontier & tangency

Lectures 04–05: combining assets gives a frontier; with a risk-free asset, every investor holds the tangency portfolio.

02

Idiosyncratic vs. systematic risk

Adding stocks removes firm-specific (idiosyncratic) risk, but only down to a floor: the market-wide (systematic) risk that no portfolio can diversify away.

03

The market portfolio → CAPM

If everyone holds the tangency portfolio, it must be the market portfolio. Only exposure to it, measured by β, earns a risk premium.

If idiosyncratic risk can be diversified away for free, should the market pay you to bear it?
No. Investors can eliminate it at no cost, so prices compensate only systematic risk. That is exactly what the CAPM says.
Part I · The model

The Capital Asset Pricing Model

CAPM · expected return of security i
E[ri] = rf + βi (E[rM] − rf)
ComponentDefinition
E[ri]Expected return of security i given by the CAPM
rfRisk-free rate
βiSensitivity of security i to the market (its systematic risk)
E[rM]Expected return of the market portfolio
E[rM] − rfMarket risk premium

In simple words

A security's risk premium, E[ri] − rf, is its beta times the market risk premium:

E[ri] − rf = βi × (E[rM] − rf)

Higher β → higher expected return. Total volatility does not enter the formula.

The market itself has β = 1, so it earns exactly the market risk premium. A stock with β = 2 should earn twice that premium; a stock with β = 0.5, half.

Part I · Measuring risk

What beta measures

Sensitivity to the market

β tells how much a stock moves, on average, when the market moves 1%.

BetaMeaningExample (5-yr β)
β > 1Riskier than the marketNVDA 2.19 · TSLA 1.76
β = 1As risky as the marketJPM 1.04 · MSFT 1.05
β < 1Less risky than the marketKO 0.29 · JNJ 0.22

Two kinds of risk

Systematic (market) risk: investors are compensated for it.
Idiosyncratic (firm-specific) risk: investors are not compensated; it can be diversified away.

Beta vs. standard deviation
Variance σ² measures total risk
It captures both systematic and idiosyncratic risk
β measures only the systematic part
Exxon Mobil (XOM): σ = 27.0%, β = 0.18.
High risk or low risk?
High total risk, low market risk. Over this period almost all of XOM's volatility was idiosyncratic (oil-specific), so under the CAPM it earns a small risk premium: 0.18 × 6% ≈ 1.1%.
Part I · Decomposition of total risk

Systematic + idiosyncratic = total

Single-index model
ri − rf = βi(rM − rf) + ei
Var(ri − rf) = Var[βi(rM − rf) + ei]
σi² = βi²σM² + σe²
βi²σM² → systematic: variance due to the market
σe² → idiosyncratic: variance independent of the market
Where the variance comes from

Presets use each stock's 5-year β and σe with σM = 15.5% (S&P 500, same period).

Part I · A new measure of risk

R²: how much of the risk is market risk?

Share of total variance explained by the market
R² = βi²σM² / σi²
0 < R² < 1 · higher R² → market risk explains most of the stock's risk
Since βi = ρi,M(σi/σM), a little algebra gives R² = ρi,M²

From our 20 stocks

AAPL 45.7% · JPM 44.4% · NVDA 46.6% · META 19.6% · KO 7.9% · XOM 1.1%

Even for large stocks, most of the variance is idiosyncratic, which a diversified portfolio removes.

Example · Vanguard Financials ETF (VFH)

VFH has β = 1.14 and volatility 15.6%. The S&P 500 has volatility 11.5%. How much of VFH's risk is due to market risk?

R² = β²σM² / σ² = 1.14² × 0.115² / 0.156²
= 0.017187 / 0.024336
R² = 0.706 = 70.6%
σe = √(0.024336 − 0.017187) = √0.007149 = 8.46%

70.6% of VFH's variance comes from the market; the rest is idiosyncratic.

Part II · Performance

Alpha and beta

Security's alpha (α)

To evaluate an investment strategy, investors compare its return with a benchmark:

α = E[rstrategy] − E[rbenchmark]

Benchmarks: the risk-free rate, the market portfolio, a buy-and-hold index strategy.

Alpha relative to the CAPM
αCAPM = E[r] − E[r]CAPM
α > 0 → given its β, the expected return is higher than the CAPM requires → undervalued
α = 0 → fairly priced
α < 0 → given its β, the expected return is lower → overvalued

Return = "beta" + alpha

The benchmark is the return earned just by taking market risk, a strategy that requires no skill (an index fund or ETF). Any return beyond it is alpha.

Return = "beta" + alpha
What is the alpha of a passive strategy?
Zero, before fees. A passive fund simply earns the benchmark. An active strategy is worth paying for only if it delivers a positive alpha.
Part II · Security Market Line

The SML: spotting mispriced stocks

The SML is the CAPM drawn in (β, E[r])

The market sits at β = 1. Points above the line (α > 0) are undervalued; points below (α < 0) are overvalued; points on the line (α = 0) are fairly priced. The line slopes up because it adjusts for beta risk.

Example · an analyst's forecasts

JPM: expected return 12%, β = 1.2 · MSFT: expected return 8%, β = 0.5 · E[rM] = 12%, rf = 2%. Is each stock over- or underpriced?

E[rJPM] = 2% + 1.2 × (12% − 2%) = 14% → α = 12% − 14% = −2% → overvalued → sell
E[rMSFT] = 2% + 0.5 × (12% − 2%) = 7% → α = 8% − 7% = +1% → undervalued → buy
Security Market Line rf = 2% · E[rM] = 12%
● Above the line: undervalued● Below the line: overvalued

Part II · Portfolios

Portfolio beta

Weighted average of the individual betas
βp = w1β1 + w2β2 + … + wNβN
The beta of cash (the risk-free asset) is zero.

Example · 50% NVDA + 50% KO

βp = 0.5 × 2.19 + 0.5 × 0.29
βp = 1.095 + 0.145 = 1.24

Zero-beta, market-neutral portfolios

Because betas add up, a portfolio can be built with no market risk: its value does not respond to market moves. Example: $100 long NVDA (β = 2.19) and $219 short an S&P 500 ETF (β = 1):

Dollar beta = 100 × 2.19 − 219 × 1.00 = 0
This is the logic behind the long/short funds in Lecture 05 (AQR): hedge the market, keep the stock-picking bet. What remains is idiosyncratic risk, and any alpha.
The story, in three ideas

From risk to price

01

Only β is priced

Diversification removes idiosyncratic risk, so the CAPM rewards only systematic risk: E[ri] = rf + βi(E[rM] − rf).

02

Decompose total risk

σ² = β²σM² + σe². R² is the share of variance explained by the market.

03

Alpha spots mispricing

α = E[r] − E[r]CAPM. Above the SML: undervalued (buy). Below: overvalued (sell).

XOM had 27% volatility but a beta of 0.18. Should investors demand a high return for holding it?
No. Under the CAPM only its market risk is priced: E[r] = 4.3% + 0.18 × 6% ≈ 5.4%. Most of its volatility is idiosyncratic and can be diversified away.
